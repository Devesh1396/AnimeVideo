Cast & style · flat, rounded, warm
  • One consistent cast across the reel. The parent watching follows one child's story from mistake to solution.
  • Recoloured per act: the same characters appear in natural colours (S1–S2), red (S3) and violet (S4), so each act still reads as one colour.
  • Indian details: school uniform and tie, mother's bindi, bun, earrings and bangles, blue-ballpoint answers, chalkboard.
  • Animated part by part: blinks, a raised hand, a ticking clock, a stress scribble, a lightbulb turning on. Everything is code, so each pose and colour can be changed quickly.
  • Type, act colours, red-pen marks and the exam paper are shared with the footage version.

Illustrated vs footage

How the two versions compare

TopicIllustrated (this)Footage version
LookWarm, friendly, one consistent cast and story; stands out from other coaching ads.Real children and parents; feels documentary and local.
TrustLess "real", but the exam paper and the problem are very recognisable.Real faces build trust quickly, if the clips look Indian and local.
Getting the visualsNothing to source; everything is drawn in code.7 clips to find or film; good Indian stock is limited.
Changes laterFast: change a pose, colour, age or outfit and re-render.Limited by what the clips show.
ReuseAarav and Mom can return in future reels as recurring characters.Each new reel needs new clips.

A hybrid also works, for example real classroom footage in S1 and illustration everywhere else.
